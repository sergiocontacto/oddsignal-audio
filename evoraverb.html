How EvoraVerb works

EvoraVerb is an algorithmic reverb plugin built around a feedback delay network. Two controls on the surface; eight delay lines, allpass diffusion and three-layer modulation underneath. This page explains what happens to your signal and how it differs from other reverbs.

Two controls, six parameters

Most reverbs give you a dozen knobs and leave the coherent combinations for you to find. EvoraVerb takes the opposite approach: Amount is a macro that rescales six internal parameters at once, along a path where every position is a usable space.

The signal chain

Each stage hands the next a wavefront that is already more scattered, so the feedback network never receives a dry impulse. It sustains and blurs a cloud that already exists, which is where the metallic character of cheaper reverbs comes from.

Pre-delay

True stereo: left and right travel down independent lines instead of being summed to mono, so the width of your source survives all the way to the tank. Third-order Lagrange interpolation, with 500 ms of headroom.

Early reflections

Eight taps per channel on separate lines, with deliberately irregular timings that differ between left and right so the image does not collapse to the centre in mono. Each bounce loses highs, the way a real wall behaves.

Allpass diffuser

Four allpass stages in series per channel, again with different times left and right. This is the stage that does most of the work removing the metallic quality: it turns the wavefront into a dense cloud before it ever reaches the tank.

FDN tank

Eight delay lines with mutually prime lengths, fed back through a Householder matrix that preserves energy and spreads every line into all the others. Left and right are injected separately per line, and the matrix itself blends the channels over time.

Damping and low-end control

Every line carries its own one-pole lowpass in the loop, each with a slightly different spectral deviation so the eight do not all decay with the same timbre. A second, very slow filter removes the DC component that would otherwise accumulate inaudibly and destabilise long tails.

What makes it different

Three-layer modulation

What gives away an artificial reverb on long tails is the periodic, predictable sway of a pure sine LFO. EvoraVerb combines three independent sources on every line:

LayerFrequencyWeightContributes
Main LFO0.15 – 0.64 Hz60%Chorus-like movement
Slow drift0.021 – 0.112 Hz28%Long-term breathing
Smoothed noiseNon-periodic12%Breaks audible repetition

Asymmetric by design

Reflection times, diffuser stages and output weights all differ between channels. Nothing is mirrored, so the stereo image stays open instead of pulling to the middle.

Built for real-time

No memory is allocated while audio is running — everything is reserved when the plugin is prepared. Every parameter is ramped, from 20 ms on Mix to 250 ms on Decay, so automation never produces a click. Denormals are handled and the feedback loop carries a soft-clip safety limiter.

An interface that gets out of the way

Vector graphics, no bitmaps, at 512 × 512. It scales cleanly and stays readable next to the rest of your session.

Frequently asked questions

Is EvoraVerb free?

Yes. Version 1.0.0 is a free public beta. Sign up through the form and you get the download.

Which formats does it support?

VST3, Audio Unit and a standalone application. No AAX, so it does not load in Pro Tools.

Does it add latency?

No. Reported latency is 0 samples, so nothing shifts in your session.

Can I use it on a mono track?

Not directly. EvoraVerb is stereo in, stereo out. For a mono source, put it on a stereo bus or a stereo send.

Why only two knobs?

Amount moves six internal parameters along a path where every position is a usable space, so there are no unusable combinations to avoid. Mix handles the balance. That is the whole surface, on purpose.

What sample rates does it support?

Whatever your host runs. Every delay length and filter coefficient is recalculated when the rate changes.

Can the parameters be automated?

Yes, both of them. Every internal parameter is ramped, so automation moves do not click.

How long can the tail get?

Up to around 12 seconds of decay at maximum Amount.
